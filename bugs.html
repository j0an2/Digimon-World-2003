Digimon World 2003 — bugs in the original game

Bugs and oddities of the original European version (SLES-03936) found by reading its decompiled code (dw3_decomp). Each entry names the code it comes from. The native PC port is built from that same code, so it has them too unless noted.

Confirmed bugs

1. Every elemental field boosts Thunder

boostFieldElement — src/fightstg/tech_action.c

The field's element is taken from the technique's icon field (boostElement = tech->icon), which is 6 (Thunder) for all seven field techniques, instead of its element. So Fire Field, Water Field, … Dark Field all boost Thunder and weaken Dark. The message still names the right element (it uses tech->element).

Fixed by: the Harmony mod (Fixed Field Moves).

2. A growth of 5 grows less than a growth of 4 (stats)

STFGTREP_raiseStats — src/stfgtrep/growth.c

Each level, a stat gains STFGTREP_statGains[tier][growth + random(0–4)]. The table has 9 columns (0–8), so a growth of 5 can ask for column 9, which doesn't exist; the PlayStation reads the first value of the next row instead, which is 0 or 1. One level-up in five, the stat that should grow the most gains almost nothing:

Levels Average gain, growth 4 Average gain, growth 5
2–4 11.4 10.0
5–19 8.8 7.6
20–39 5.6 4.8
40–59 4.6 4.0
60–79 3.0 2.6

From level 80 the last row reads past the whole table, into the resistance gains, whose first value is also 0.

Affected Rookies: Kumamon (Strength and Defense), Renamon (Spirit), Monmon (Speed), Veemon (Wisdom and Charisma). The very stats each was designed to excel at grow about 13% slower than a 4 would.

3. A growth of 5 grows less than a growth of 4 (resistances)

STFGTREP_raiseStats — src/stfgtrep/growth.c

Up to level 40, each resistance gains STFGTREP_resistGains[growth + random(0–3)], a table of 8 values (0 0 0 1 1 1 2 2). A growth of 5 can ask for value 8, past the end; on the disc the next word is 0. A growth of 5 averages 1.25 a level, a growth of 4 1.5.

Seven of the eight Rookies have one resistance with growth 5: Kotemon (Water), Monmon (Machine), Agumon (Fire), Veemon (Ice), Guilmon (Wind), Renamon (Thunder), Patamon (Dark). Kumamon has none.

4. Magic without an element reads the target's Speed as a resistance

FIGHTSTG_rollMagicCritical — src/fightstg/rolls.c

The magic critical chance is min(64, ElementStrength × 100 / resist[element − 2]) + 4. For a technique with no element, element − 2 is −1, and the value read is the one stored just before the resistances: the target's Speed. So the critical chance of element-less magic depends on the target's Speed:

  • Most of these techniques (the status and drain spells) have an element strength of 1: 100 / Speed is about 0–2, for a 3–5% critical chance.
  • Gate of Destiny (64), KongouKaimandara (72) and Back Storm (72) have a high element strength but no element: against a target with Speed up to about 100 they reach the 53% cap (64 × 100 / 100 = 64), and 28% at Speed 200.

5. The Shock Horn never paralyzes

FIGHTSTG_applyTechEffect — src/fightstg/tech_effects.c; WEAPON_DATA — src/main/game/items.c

"Horn that paralyzes the body", but its record has a status chance of 0 and a strength of 0. The plain attack only tries a weapon's status when that chance isn't 0 (if (stats[0].statuses[HIT_PARALYSIS].chance) FIGHTSTG_tryParalysis()), so the Shock Horn never paralyzes: it is a horn of Strength +90 and nothing else. The other status weapons have 24–64/128.

Oddities

Behaviour that looks unintended, or at least surprising, but may be by design.

  • Debuffs last according to the target's Spirit, not the user's. FIGHTSTG_queueBoostEnd times a boost by the Spirit of the Digimon it is on. For a buff that is the user; for a debuff (Armor Break, AncientGreymon…) it is the enemy, so debuffs last longer on high-Spirit enemies.
  • The field elements the code weakens don't match the descriptions. "Fire Field: strengthen fire, weaken ice", but FIGHTSTG_opposedElements makes the Fire Field weaken Water (Ice Field weakens Fire, and so on). See FORMULAS.md.
  • A field never boosts an accessory's element. FIGHTSTG_getElementBoost only looks at the technique's element, so a basic attack with Fire Power gets nothing from a Fire Field.
  • A critical-chance item lowers accuracy. In FIGHTSTG_rollHit the player's level difference has the critical bonus taken off it, so Search Crest, Saber Fang or Shuriken cost some accuracy.
  • Multi-hit techniques roll one critical for all their hits: all hits are critical, or none.
  • No minimum damage: a hit can do 0.
  • Two Digimon share more digivolution EXP than one. STFGTREP_getDigimonExp gives the forms used in a battle 100% of the DV EXP if one was used, 60% each if two (120% in all) and an equal split if three or more. Using exactly two forms a battle earns the most DV EXP.
  • Running away starts hard: the first attempt has a base chance of 8/128 (6%), the second 16/128, and so on, plus the level and Speed differences (FIGHTSTG_testRunAway).

Fixed in the European version

  • Field gauges (src/fieldstg/data/fieldstg.c): the USA version has no row 8 in FIELDSTG_gaugeRows and reads past the table (a null pointer) once GAME.randomGauges is used up. The European version adds the row.

  • An asleep enemy could run away (USA). The European FIGHTSTG_testRunAway stops it.

  • The Counter Crest countered every hit (USA). The European version rolls 25% + 50% × damage / max HP (FIGHTSTG_rollCounter) before the counterattack.

Latent

  • Boost slot 3 (FIGHTSTG_boostStats): a fourth boost would be worked out from Attack, added to an enemy's Spirit, and ignored for partners. No technique uses it.

Reported elsewhere

  • Picking Claw and Snapping Claw when the enemy is knocked out: listed as fixed by the Harmony mod; its cause is not traced here.
